# Trust and Communities

This page explains two of the most important features of Polaris: how trust works and how communities function.

## Trust Scores

On most social networks, the most important number is your follower count. The more followers you have, the louder your voice is. This means popular people (even if they don't know what they're talking about) get heard more than experts.

On Polaris, we use **trust scores** instead.

Everyone on Polaris has a trust score between 0 and 10. The more people trust you, the higher your score. Here's how it works:

### How Trust Connections Work

You can mark any user as someone you **trust** or someone you **don't trust** by visiting their profile. This is completely private &mdash; nobody else can see who you trust or don't trust.

When many people trust someone, that person's trust score goes up. When many people don't trust them, it goes down.

Think of it like asking your friends: "Do you know this person? Are they reliable?" If lots of people say yes, you'd probably trust them too.

### Why Trust Scores Matter

Trust scores affect several things:

- **Post visibility**: In some feed views, posts from highly trusted users appear more prominently
- **Community membership**: Some communities may require a minimum trust score to join
- **Moderation**: Users with higher trust scores have more weight in moderation decisions

### How to Earn Trust

The best way to earn trust on Polaris is simple: **be a good person**.

- Share thoughtful, honest content
- Treat others with respect even when you disagree
- Be consistent and reliable in what you say
- Add helpful community notes
- Participate constructively in communities

Trust takes time to build. Don't worry if your score starts low &mdash; everyone starts somewhere.

### Can You Lose Trust?

Yes. If you consistently behave in ways that make people not trust you, your score will go down. This isn't a punishment from an administrator &mdash; it's the honest opinion of the community. If you change your behavior, people may start trusting you again over time.

## Communities

Communities are groups of people who share a common interest. Think of them like neighborhoods in a city. Each one has its own culture, its own rules, and its own way of doing things.

### Finding Communities

You can browse all public communities from the Communities page. Each community shows:

- Its name and description
- How many members it has
- The community's rules (set by the moderators)
- Whether it's open to everyone or invite-only

### Creating a Community

Anyone can create a community. To do so:

1. Click the "Create Community" button on the Communities page
2. Give your community a name, description, and rules
3. Set whether it's open or invite-only
4. Choose optional tags to help people find it

### Slow Boot Mode

New communities start in **slow boot** mode. This means they accept new members slowly at first. This gives the community time to figure out what kind of place it wants to be before it grows too fast.

During slow boot, new members are added gradually. The community can be promoted out of slow boot once it's proven to be healthy and well-run.

### Forking a Community

Sometimes a community might drift away from what you wanted it to be. Instead of fighting about it, you can **fork** the community. Forking creates a copy of the community's members and rules, giving you a fresh start with a new direction.

The original community continues as before. Both communities can grow independently.

### Community Rules

Each community can have its own rules on top of the global Polaris rules. When you join a community, you agree to follow both sets of rules. Moderators (chosen by the community) enforce the community-specific rules.

If someone breaks a community rule, the moderators can take action &mdash; but serious violations go to a jury of users (see [Moderation and Safety](04-moderation.md)).

### Invite-Only Communities

Some communities are invite-only. This means you need an invitation from an existing member to join. This is useful for communities that want to stay small and focused, or for private groups.

If you want to join an invite-only community, ask a member to invite you.

## Community Notes

Community notes are not just for posts &mdash; they also work in communities. If someone shares something misleading in a community, you can add a note providing accurate information. Other community members vote on whether the note is helpful.
