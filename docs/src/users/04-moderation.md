# Moderation and Safety

Threadlight has a moderation system that works differently from most social networks.
Instead of a small group of people making all the decisions, **everyone shares the
responsibility** of keeping the platform healthy.

## The Rules

Threadlight has four rules. They are short on purpose.

### Rule 1: Do not deny that another person is a human being.

You do not have to like someone. You do not have to agree with their politics. But you
must treat them as a person. No dehumanizing language. No advocating violence against
a group. No organizing harassment against an individual.

### Rule 2: Do not lie about things that cause real harm.

Opinions are fine. Interpretations are fine. But denying observable reality when that
denial causes concrete harm is not allowed. This includes Holocaust denial, anti-vaccine
misinformation, climate change denial used to block action, and conspiracy theories that
lead to real-world violence.

### Rule 3: Do not fake being a real person.

Every account should be a real human being communicating in good faith. Bots, bought
engagement, coordinated fake accounts, and manufactured popularity are not allowed.

### Rule 4: Do not post illegal content.

Whatever is against the law where the server is physically located is not allowed here.

## M1: Content Moderation (First Tier)

The first tier of moderation is done by regular users. Anyone at **Regular** trust level
or above can rate a post with a label:

- **Helpful labels:** Insightful, Interesting, Informative, Funny, Underrated
- **Corrective labels:** Misleading, Off-Topic, Troll, Flamebait

Labeling a post does not delete it. It adds context. A post rated "Misleading" by enough
people will be visually marked and will appear less in feeds. A post rated "Insightful"
will be seen by more people.

Each moderator gets a limited number of actions per day (usually 5 to 10). This prevents
any single person from having too much influence. Unused actions expire at the end of
the day.

### Reporting

If you see something that clearly breaks the rules and you are not a moderator, you can
report it. Reports go into a queue for review.

## M2: Meta-Moderation (Second Tier)

The second tier moderates the moderators themselves. Every so often, moderators are asked
to review a decision made by another moderator.

You see the post and the label that was applied. You are asked one simple question:

**"Was this moderation fair?"**

You answer Yes or No. If a moderator consistently makes decisions that other moderators
think are unfair, they lose their moderation privileges. If they are consistently fair,
they may earn a slightly larger daily budget.

### No Appeals

Once a post is labeled, the label stays. There is no appeals process. This prevents the
system from becoming a game where people try to argue their way out of consequences.
Instead, moderators are careful because their own reputation is on the line.

## Admin Safety

For very serious cases — real-world threats, organized harassment, illegal content —
the server administrators have direct tools. They can issue warnings, suspend accounts,
or permanently ban users.

All admin actions are recorded in a **public moderation log.** Anyone can see what
action was taken and why. This keeps the administrators accountable to the community.

## Your Safety Tools

You have full control over your own experience:

- **Block** someone — they cannot see your posts or interact with you
- **Mute** someone — same as block but temporary, and they do not know they are muted
- **Content filters** — hide posts that contain certain words or topics
- **Privacy settings** — control who can message you and who can see your profile

## What to Do If You Are Being Harassed

1. Block the person immediately.
2. Report the harassing content using the report button.
3. Do not respond — replying often makes things worse.
4. Contact an administrator if the harassment continues through other means.

Harassment violates Rule 1. Repeat offenders face consequences that are recorded in the
public moderation log.
