# Moderation and Safety

Polaris takes a different approach to moderation. Instead of giving all the power to a few people, **moderation is shared by the community**. This page explains how it works and how you can stay safe.

## How Moderation is Different Here

On most social networks, a small group of moderators or employees make all the decisions about what's allowed. This gives them a lot of power &mdash; power that can be abused.

On Polaris, moderation decisions are made by **juries of regular users**. When someone breaks the rules, a group of randomly selected users review what happened and decide what to do. This prevents any single person from having too much control.

## The Rules

Polaris has four rules. They are deliberately minimal. If you are a decent person trying to
have a real conversation, you will not have trouble with any of them.

### Rule 1: You must not deny the humanity of another person.

All humans deserve a baseline of dignity. You do not have to like someone, agree with their
politics, or respect their choices. But you must recognize them as a person &mdash; not a
cause, not a symbol, not a target.

This means no dehumanization. No advocating for violence against a group. No systematic
harassment designed to drive someone off the platform. No organizing harm against a person
or category of people.

The line: you can say "I disagree with X policy" or "X idea is wrong." You cannot say "X
people don't deserve to exist" or organize a campaign of abuse against an individual.

### Rule 2: You must not lie about material conditions.

You are free to have opinions. You are free to interpret facts differently. You are not free
to deny observable reality when that denial causes concrete harm.

This means no Holocaust denial, no "vaccines cause autism" pseudoscience, no climate change
denial that delays action, no conspiracy theories that lead to real-world violence.

The line: fun speculation and humor are fine. Organizing around a demonstrable falsehood
that hurts real people is not.

### Rule 3: You must not astroturf.

Every account should be a real person communicating in good faith. You may not use bots,
buy engagement, coordinate sock-puppet campaigns, or manufacture artificial consensus.

We want to know what people actually think. Not what a PR operation wants us to think.

The line: a personal project that uses automation (like a reminder bot) is fine if labeled
clearly. An army of accounts designed to simulate popular support is not.

### Rule 4: You must not post illegal content.

Whatever is illegal in the jurisdiction where the server is physically located is not
allowed here. We do not want to be raided.

## Reporting a Post

If you see something that breaks the rules:

1. Click the three dots on the post
2. Select "Report"
3. Choose why you're reporting it (from a list of reasons)
4. Submit the report

Your report is anonymous &mdash; the person you reported won't know it was you.

## How Juries Work

When someone is reported, a jury is formed. Here's the process:

1. **A case is opened.** The reported content is reviewed to see if it clearly breaks the rules.
2. **Jurors are selected.** A small group of random users (who have reached the appropriate onboarding stage) are asked to serve on the jury.
3. **Evidence is presented.** The reporter's claim and the reported person's response are both shown to the jury.
4. **Jurors vote.** Each juror votes on what should happen:
   - **Dismiss** &mdash; no rules were broken
   - **Warning** &mdash; a minor violation, give a warning
   - **Suspension** &mdash; temporary ban (duration depends on severity)
   - **Ban** &mdash; permanent removal from the server
5. **The decision is carried out.** If enough jurors agree on a punishment, it's applied.

### Serving on a Jury

If you're selected for a jury, you'll get a notification. Serving on a jury is a responsibility. Please take it seriously:

- Read all the evidence carefully
- Consider both sides
- Vote honestly based on the rules, not on whether you like the person

Users who serve on juries thoughtfully earn trust points. Users who vote carelessly or maliciously may lose trust.

## Your Safety Tools

Polaris gives you several tools to control your own experience:

### Blocking Users

If someone is bothering you, you can block them. Blocked users cannot:

- See your posts
- Reply to your posts
- Send you messages
- Follow you

To block someone, go to their profile and click "Block."

### Content Filters

You can create custom filters to hide posts that contain certain words or topics. This is useful if there are subjects you'd rather not see.

To set up filters, go to your settings and add words or phrases to your filter list.

### Muting

Muting someone is like a temporary block. They won't know they've been muted, but their posts won't appear in your feed. You can unmute them at any time.

### Privacy Settings

In your settings, you can control:

- **Email visibility**: Whether your email is shown on your profile
- **Profile visibility**: Who can see your profile and posts
- **DM settings**: Who can send you direct messages

## What to Do If You're Being Harassed

If someone is harassing you:

1. **Block them** immediately to stop further contact
2. **Report their posts** using the report function
3. **Don't engage** &mdash; replying often makes things worse
4. **Tell a moderator** if the harassment continues through other means

Remember: harassment is against the rules. The jury system is there to protect you. Use it.

## Appeals

If you were punished by a jury and you think the decision was wrong, you can appeal. Appeals are reviewed by a different set of users who weren't on the original jury. If they agree the punishment was unfair, it can be undone.
