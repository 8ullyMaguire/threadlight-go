# Moderation and Safety

Threadlight uses a layered moderation system. Content moderation is shared by the community
(M1), moderators are held accountable through meta-moderation (M2), and administrators have
safety tools for the most serious cases.

## The Rules

Threadlight has four rules. They are deliberately minimal.

### Rule 1: You must not deny the humanity of another person.

No dehumanization, no advocating violence against a group, no systematic harassment.

### Rule 2: You must not lie about material conditions.

Opinions are fine. Denying observable reality when that denial causes concrete harm is not.
This covers Holocaust denial, anti-vaccine pseudoscience, climate denial, and conspiracy
theories that lead to real-world violence.

### Rule 3: You must not astroturf.

Every account should be a real person. Bots, bought engagement, coordinated sock-puppet
campaigns, and manufactured consensus are not allowed.

### Rule 4: You must not post illegal content.

Whatever is illegal in the server's jurisdiction is not allowed here.

## M1: Content Moderation

Any user at **Regular** trust level or above can moderate a post by applying a label:

- **Positive labels**: Insightful, Interesting, Informative, Funny, Underrated
- **Corrective labels**: Misleading, Off-Topic, Troll, Flamebait

Labeling is not removal — it adds context. A post rated "Misleading" by enough moderators
will be visually marked and its feed prominence decreased. A post widely rated "Insightful"
gains visibility.

Moderators receive a limited budget of actions per day (typically 5-10). Unused budget does
not roll over.

## M2: Meta-Moderation

Every few hours, moderators are asked to review a recent moderation decision by another
moderator. The question is always the same: **"Was this moderation fair?"**

If a moderator's decisions are consistently rated unfair, they lose their moderation
privileges. Fair moderators may earn a slightly larger daily budget over time.

There are no appeals for individual moderation labels. Once applied, the label sticks.
This prevents the system from becoming a meta-game.

## Admin-Guided Safety Enforcement

Administrators have tools for cases the community system cannot handle:

- **Auto-flagging**: Hate speech and abuse patterns are automatically flagged for admin
  review, not removed — a human always decides.
- **Warnings, suspensions, bans**: Administrators can issue direct sanctions for serious
  violations, particularly when a user poses a real-world threat or is engaged in organized
  harassment. These actions are public (see below).
- **Admin override**: In rare cases, an admin can override a moderation decision. Overrides
  are logged publicly with a justification.

### Public Moderation Log

All administrative actions (warnings, suspensions, bans, overrides) are recorded in a
**public moderation log** visible from the server's about page. Each entry shows:

- The action taken
- A public justification (no private user data)
- The date

This ensures administrators are accountable to the community in the same way that moderators
are accountable through M2.

## Your Safety Tools

- **Block** — the blocked user cannot see or interact with you
- **Mute** — temporary block, the user is not notified
- **Content filters** — hide posts containing specific words or topics
- **Privacy settings** — control who can message you and see your profile
- **Report** — the reported content enters the M1 queue

## What to Do If You Are Being Harassed

1. Block the person immediately
2. Report the harassing content
3. Do not engage
4. Contact an administrator if the harassment continues through other means

Harassment violates Rule 1. Repeat offenders face account suspension visible in the public
moderation log.
