# Introduction

Welcome to Threadlight. Let us start at the beginning.

## What Is Threadlight?

Threadlight is a place on the internet where people can talk to each other without being manipulated, without being yelled at by strangers, and without some invisible computer program deciding what they should see.

You know how most social media works. You open the app and it shows you something that makes you angry. You scroll, you get angrier, you scroll more. The company that made the app makes money because you keep looking at the screen. They do not care if you are happy. They just care that you are still there.

Threadlight is not like that.

There are no ads. There is no algorithm that tries to make you angry on purpose. There are no billionaires deciding what you should think about. There is no hate speech dressed up as engagement.

On Threadlight, you decide what you see. You decide who to trust. You decide what matters.

Think of other social networks like a giant shopping mall where speakers blast ads at you from every direction. Think of Threadlight like a cozy neighborhood coffee shop where you know the people, you trust what they say, and you can talk freely without being screamed at.

## Why Does Threadlight Exist?

Because the people who own almost everything — the people who own the buildings you live in, the people who own the factories where your clothes are made, the people who decide whether you still have a job tomorrow — also own the social networks you use every day.

And they have figured something out.

If they can make you hate the person who lives next door, you will not notice that they are the ones who set your rent. If they can make you angry at someone on the other side of the country who uses different words than you do, you will not notice that your school's budget just got cut again.

The algorithm on other social networks does not show you things that are true or useful. It shows you things that make you angry at the right people. Not the people who actually have power over your life. The person who lives ten miles away but watches a different news channel. The person who uses different pronouns. The person who goes to a different church.

This is not an accident. It is how those networks are designed to work.

Threadlight was built to work differently. We do not want to make you angry. We want to give you a place to talk, to share, to learn, and to connect with people who care about the same things you care about.

## Who Is This For?

Threadlight is for anyone who has ever felt tired of being yelled at by an algorithm — anyone who wants to have real conversations instead of shouting matches. It is for people who think social media should bring everyone together instead of tearing them apart. It is for folks who believe, deep down, that the people running the big social networks do not have our best interests in mind, and that we deserve something better. If any of that sounds like you, then this place is for you.

## How Threadlight Works

Threadlight is built on a few simple ideas that work together, and none of them involve letting a corporation decide what you see.

The first idea is trust levels. The longer you are here and the more you participate in good faith, the more you can do. You earn trust over time, not by being loud. This means that the person who has been a thoughtful member of the community for a year has more say than someone who showed up yesterday just to cause trouble.

The second idea is vouching for each other. When someone who has been here a while says "I trust this person," they put their own reputation on the line. This makes it very hard for fake accounts and bots to take over, because a stranger cannot simply appear and claim to be trustworthy — they need the community to vouch for them. It is collective power in action.

The third idea is that everyone helps with moderation. Instead of a small group of people deciding what is allowed and what is not, everyone can rate posts as helpful or misleading. And the people who do the rating are themselves rated by others. This creates a system of shared responsibility and transparency, where no single person or small group holds all the power.

The fourth idea is tagging together. Nobody owns the tags. Anyone can add a tag to any post, and the community votes on which tags fit best. It is a small example of community ownership — the people decide what makes sense, not a board of directors.

The fifth idea is that your feed follows your rules. The things you see are based on who you trust and what you care about, not what makes the most money for a corporation. You are not a product. You are a person.
