# Introduction

Welcome to Polaris! This guide will help you understand what Polaris is, how it works, and how you can be part of it.

## What is Polaris?

Polaris is a social network. But not the kind you're used to.

On most social networks, an invisible computer program &mdash; an "algorithm" &mdash; decides what you see. It shows you things that make you angry, scared, or excited, because those things keep you scrolling. The longer you scroll, the more money the company makes.

Polaris is different. There is no algorithm deciding what you see. There are no ads tracking your every move. There is no billionaire pulling the strings behind the scenes.

**You decide what you see. You decide who to trust. You decide what matters.**

Think of other social networks like a giant mall with loud speakers blasting ads at you from every direction. Polaris is more like a cozy neighborhood coffee shop where you know the people, trust their opinions, and can speak freely without being screamed at by strangers.

## The Core Idea

Polaris is built on a simple belief: **you are smart enough to decide for yourself.**

Most social networks treat you like a puppet. They nudge you this way and that way, trying to make you feel certain emotions so you'll stay on their site longer. They divide people into teams and encourage you to hate the other team because hatred is addictive.

We think that's harmful &mdash; both to you and to society.

On Polaris, the tools are designed to help you think clearly, not to manipulate you. You choose what content you see. You choose who you trust. When something breaks the rules, regular users like you decide what happens next &mdash; not some anonymous employee at a corporation.

## Who is This For?

Polaris is for anyone who:

- Is tired of being manipulated by algorithms
- Wants to have real conversations, not shouting matches
- Believes they can think for themselves
- Wants to be part of communities that actually care about their members
- Thinks social media should bring people together, not tear them apart

## A Note on Philosophy

Polaris was built because we noticed something strange about the internet.

The same people who own almost everything &mdash; the landlords, the shareholder classes, the
people who decide whether you keep your job, the executives who ship production to the lowest-wage
dictatorship &mdash; also own the social networks you use every day. And they've figured out
something useful: if they can make you hate the person next to you, you won't notice who's
picking your pocket.

That's what the algorithm does. It doesn't show you things that are true or useful. It shows
you things that make you angry at the right people &mdash; the people it's decided are your
enemies. Not the people who set your rent. Not the people who voted to cut your school's
budget. The person on the other side of a culture war. The person who uses different pronouns.
The person who lives ten miles away but watches a different news channel.

**This is not an accident. It is a feature.**

There is a name for the pattern that emerges when a society gets divided this way. Some call
it the **horseshoe theory**: the far left and far right bend toward each other like a
horseshoe, developing the same authoritarian habits with different branding.

What does that look like in practice?

On the right, it shows up as nativist scapegoating: "they" are taking your jobs, "they" are
destroying your culture, a strongman leader will fix everything. The enemy is always an
outsider. The solution is always force.

On the left, it shows up as moral enforcement: cancel anyone who says the wrong thing,
demand ideological purity, reduce every person to their group identity. The enemy is always
a heretic. The solution is always exclusion.

Both sides do the same thing: they tell you to stop thinking and start fighting. They tell
you that your problems are caused by a group of bad people, not by a system that concentrates
wealth and power at the top. They turn your neighbor into an enemy so you don't notice who's
actually looting your future.

Beneath the surface of this apparent symmetry, the material reality reveals a different
dynamic: the performative moralism and linguistic policing of the so-called woke left are
not a rival to fascism but a distinctly bourgeois diversion &mdash; a petty-bourgeois
identity politics that fragments the working class along racial and gender lines, diverting
economic grievances into cultural feuds that serve the ruling elite's need for social control.
The open hatred and nativist rage of the far-right, by contrast, are the raw, coercive arm
of capital in systemic crisis, preparing the ground for authoritarian terror when liberal
democracy can no longer manage the fundamental contradiction between labor and ownership.
Both phenomena, in practice, function to suppress the core conflict of class: one through
ideological conformity enforced by social penalties and manufactured consent, the other
through paramilitary violence, scapegoating, and the dismantling of worker protections.
Neither cancels the other in any meaningful sense; rather, they operate as complementary
valves of bourgeois hegemony &mdash; the former pacifying dissent by absorbing activists
into a fractured identity hierarchy, the latter threatening extermination for those who
refuse to submit. Together, they obscure the only genuine universal interest &mdash; the
collective emancipation of the many from the few &mdash; which neither cultural censorship
nor ethnic hatred can ultimately contain, for both ultimately defend the same property
relations that breed all such division.

**Polaris stands against all of it.**

We believe that the people who work for a living &mdash; whether they live in a city or a
small town, whether they vote blue or red, whether they go to church or they don't &mdash;
have more in common with each other than they do with the people at the very top. We believe
that dividing people by identity is the oldest trick in the ruling class's book. We believe
you should judge ideas by whether they're true, not by who said them.

You don't have to agree with any of this to use Polaris. But this is the reason it exists.
Not to make money. Not to grow. To give people a place to talk without being played.

There are 138 million children in the world today being exploited for their labor &mdash;
working in mines, in factories, in fields, in homes where they are not safe. Many of those
children are making the products that fill the stores of the countries that bomb their
neighborhoods. If you are not bothered by this, we would prefer you do not use this platform.
